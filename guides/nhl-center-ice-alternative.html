NHL Center Ice Alternative

Hockey fans who've moved away from their team's home market know the drill: either pay for a dedicated out of market package or miss a lot of games. IPTV is one alternative people bring up, so let's look at how it actually stacks up against Center Ice.

What Center Ice is for

NHL Center Ice is the league's out of market package, typically offered through cable and satellite providers, built to carry games your regional sports network doesn't show locally. It's a focused product aimed at one audience: people who moved, or whose favorite team just isn't on their local feed on a given night. If that's your exact situation, a dedicated package built for it has an obvious appeal.

How IPTV handles the same need

IPTV takes a different route. Instead of a single rights package tied to one league, you get an internet delivered channel bundle, usually covering national sports networks, regional feeds depending on the provider, plus news and entertainment channels on top. Hockey is part of that bundle rather than the entire point of it. For someone who wants a game on in the background most nights along with everything else they'd normally watch, that can be a more useful setup day to day, even without being purpose built around the NHL.

We should be upfront here too: an IPTV package can't promise every single out of market game the way a dedicated rights product can. Which channels are included and what they choose to air shifts over time and varies between providers. We'd rather say that clearly than let you assume otherwise.

Getting it running on your TV

The setup itself is simple. Choose a device, a Firestick, an Android TV box, a smart TV with its own app store, a phone, or a tablet, download a player app such as TiviMate or IPTV Smarters Pro, and enter your login details. Most people are watching within ten minutes of getting set up. Our devices page covers which hardware tends to work best for different TVs, and our apps comparison breaks down the differences between the player apps if you're deciding between them.

Center Ice, by contrast, typically comes bundled through your existing cable or satellite box, so setup there is really a matter of calling your provider and adding the package rather than configuring a new app.

A fair look at pricing

Center Ice has generally run around $150 to $180 for the season as an add on through a cable or satellite provider. Spread across the roughly seven-month regular season, October through April, that's about $21 to $26 a month. It's usually billed on top of an existing cable or satellite plan rather than as a standalone product, so the real cost to add it depends partly on what base package you're already paying for.

Our plans are a flat one-time payment regardless of sport or season: $54.99 for 6 months (about $9.17 a month) or $79.99 for 12 months (about $6.67 a month), both lower than Center Ice's monthly rate. The difference is that Center Ice is a dedicated rights package built to carry out of market games specifically, while a broad channel lineup includes hockey as one piece of a much bigger bundle, with no promise that a given team's game lands on a network your provider carries that night.

Hockey's real problem: fragmentation across regional apps

Hockey's out of market issue isn't really a blackout rule the way baseball's or basketball's is. It's distribution fragmentation: a growing number of NHL teams' local broadcasts now live behind that team's own regional sports network and its own standalone streaming app, rather than one unified national package you can subscribe to once. That means following three or four different teams closely can mean juggling three or four different regional apps and subscriptions, on top of whatever national package covers the rest of the league. Center Ice helps with the national and out of market side of that picture, but it was never built to solve the patchwork of team-by-team regional apps either.

A broad IPTV channel lineup doesn't solve that patchwork perfectly, since it depends on which regional and national sports networks a given provider carries, but it does put a wider set of those networks under one login instead of a separate app and subscription per team. For someone following several teams across different regions, consolidating that many logins into one place is often the bigger practical win, more than any blackout workaround.

Picture quality and buffering, set straight

Stream quality on IPTV ranges from standard definition up to full HD depending on the channel and provider, with 4K on demand content becoming more common but not yet standard across live sports. Center Ice, running through your existing cable or satellite box, usually holds a more consistent picture since it isn't dependent on your home internet at all. If you do switch to an internet-based option, buffering almost always traces back to your own connection rather than the stream itself, and a wired line to your streaming device matters more on a full slate night, when several of the league's games are on at once across different apps in the same house.

Who actually benefits from switching

If you watch one team religiously and want every single one of their out of market games with no gaps, a dedicated package like Center Ice is probably worth keeping around despite the cost. If hockey is one of several things you follow, alongside other sports, the news, and something to put on for the family, a broader IPTV package tends to make more sense for the household as a whole.

What happens if you travel during the season

One thing cable based packages like Center Ice run into is that they're tied to a specific box in a specific house. If you're away from home for a stretch, a work trip, visiting family, a month somewhere warmer in February, you're often just not watching unless the location has the same service set up. An IPTV subscription tends to travel better since it's tied to your login rather than a physical box, so the same app and credentials generally work wherever you have an internet connection, within whatever device limits your plan allows. That's not a hockey specific feature, but it matters more for hockey fans than most, given how many games get missed to road trips and winter travel.

Checking the schedule without a second app

Most IPTV player apps include a built in program guide, so you can see what's airing across your channels without pulling up a separate website or app just to check start times. It's a small thing, but after a season of flipping between a schedule site and your TV, having both in one place is the kind of detail that ends up mattering more than it sounds like on paper.

Frequently asked questions

Is IPTV the same thing as NHL Center Ice?

No. Center Ice is a dedicated out of market hockey package. IPTV is a broader channel package delivered over the internet, where hockey is one part of a much larger lineup rather than the whole product.

What do I need to set up IPTV for hockey?

A streaming device such as a Firestick, Android TV box, or smart TV app, a player app like TiviMate or IPTV Smarters Pro, your login details, and a stable internet connection.

Will I get every out of market hockey game?

Not guaranteed. Hockey's bigger issue is fragmentation: a growing number of teams' local broadcasts live behind that team's own regional sports network app rather than one unified national package. A broad channel lineup can bring more of those networks under one login, but it won't cover every team's regional feed on its own.

Can more than one person in my house stream at the same time?

Yes, depending on your plan's simultaneous stream limit. Multi-device plans are available for households that need more than one screen running.

Can I try before paying for a season?
