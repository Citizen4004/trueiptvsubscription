How to Set Up TiviMate

TiviMate is a favorite for its remote-friendly program guide, the kind that feels more like a cable box than a typical app menu. Getting your subscription loaded into it takes about two minutes.

Step by step

  1. Install the app. On Firestick, search the Amazon Appstore for "TiviMate." On Android or Android TV, grab it from Google Play.
  2. Open the playlist screen. First time opening TiviMate, it prompts you to add a playlist right away. Used it before? Go to Settings → Playlists → Add Playlist.
  3. Pick your login type. Choose Xtream Codes Login if you've got a server URL, username, and password, or M3U Playlist for a single link.
  4. Type in your details. Enter them exactly as provided, a stray space or typo is the most common reason this step fails.
  5. Wait for sync. TiviMate pulls your channels and guide. Once it's done, you're browsing from the main screen.

Is Premium worth paying for?

The free tier already covers full live playback, the program guide, and basic favorites, genuinely enough for most people. Premium unlocks a handful of specific extras: multi-screen viewing so you can watch more than one channel at a time, a PIN-protected parental lock, custom channel logos and colors, and cloud backup so a new device doesn't mean rebuilding your setup from scratch. It's worth the upgrade mainly if one of those specific features matters to you, not as a blanket "better version" of the app.

Favorites and multiple profiles

Press and hold on any channel in the guide to add it to Favorites, a shortcut tab worth setting up once your channel list gets long enough to make scrolling annoying. Separately, under Settings → Profiles, TiviMate lets different people in the house keep their own favorites and channel order without stepping on each other's setup, all while pulling from the same underlying subscription.

A few shortcuts worth knowing

Know a channel's number? Type it on your remote to jump straight there instead of scrolling. Press and hold on a channel in the guide for quick info and options without leaving the screen. And while you're browsing ahead in the guide, TiviMate keeps the currently playing channel running in a small preview window rather than freezing on a static list, so you don't lose track of what's actually on.

Organizing channels into categories

If your channel list arrives as one long undivided block, TiviMate lets you group channels into custom categories under Settings → Channels → Edit Channels. Select a batch of channels and assign them to a category you name yourself, like "Sports" or "Kids," and that grouping shows up as its own tab in the guide. It's a one-time setup that makes a big difference once you're past the first few dozen channels.

Backing up your setup

Even without Premium's cloud backup, you can export your TiviMate configuration manually from Settings → Backup/Restore, saving a file to local storage or a connected cloud drive. It's worth doing once your favorites and categories are set up the way you like, so a factory reset or a new device doesn't mean starting from zero.

Understanding the EPG time window

TiviMate's guide typically loads a rolling window of program data, often somewhere around 24 to 48 hours depending on what your provider's EPG source sends. If you're trying to check what's on three days from now and the guide comes up blank for that far out, that's expected, not a bug, the data simply isn't published that far in advance by most sources. Checking back closer to the date usually fills it in.

Casting content from a phone versus using the native app

Some people get used to casting TiviMate's picture from a phone to a TV using Chromecast rather than installing the native TV app. It works, but it's a noticeably worse experience day to day: you lose the full-screen remote-friendly guide, battery drains faster on the phone, and the stream has to survive an extra hop over your Wi-Fi. If your TV or streaming box can run TiviMate natively, as Firestick and Android TV both can, installing it directly is worth the extra five minutes over relying on casting as your everyday setup.

TiviMate on a Smart TV without a separate box

TiviMate itself is built for Android-based platforms, so it doesn't install directly on Samsung's Tizen or LG's webOS the way a native Smart TV app does. If you're specifically on a Samsung or LG TV without an Android TV box or Firestick attached, something like Smart IPTV, which does run natively on those operating systems, is a better starting point than trying to force TiviMate onto hardware it wasn't built for. Frequently asked questions

Does TiviMate support Xtream Codes?

Yes, alongside M3U playlist URLs, both are available when you add a new playlist.

Is TiviMate free to use?

The free version covers live playback, the program guide, and favorites. A paid Premium tier adds multi-screen viewing, a parental PIN lock, custom channel art, and cloud backup of your settings.

Why is my program guide empty in TiviMate?

Usually the guide data just hasn't synced yet. Refresh the playlist under Settings → Playlists, or wait a few minutes.

Can each person in my household have their own favorites?

Yes, TiviMate supports multiple profiles on one installation, each with separate favorites and channel ordering, sharing the same subscription.

Can I add more than one playlist to TiviMate?

Yes. TiviMate supports multiple playlists on a single install, useful if you ever need to combine more than one subscription in the same app.

Does TiviMate work without an internet connection?

No. Live channels stream directly from your provider's servers in real time, so an active internet connection is required the entire time you're watching, there's no offline or downloaded playback mode.

Keeping TiviMate up to date

Since TiviMate is sideloaded through the Appstore directly on Firestick rather than through Downloader, it typically updates itself in the background the same way other Appstore apps do. On Android and Android TV through Google Play, the same applies as long as auto-updates are enabled on the device. It's still worth an occasional manual check, a version lagging a release or two behind can sometimes explain a guide quirk or a settings option that doesn't match what a newer guide describes.
