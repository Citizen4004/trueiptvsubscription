How Much Does It Cost to Watch NHL Without Cable?

An 82-game regular season plus a long playoff run means hockey fans end up paying for TV access for most of the year. Here's what that costs on cable compared to an IPTV subscription.

What cable costs for hockey coverage

Hockey coverage on cable usually sits inside a mid-tier package or above, meaning you're buying a bundle of channels to get the handful that actually carry your team's games. In a lot of regions, there's also a separate regional sports network fee added on top of the package price, a charge that shows up as its own line even though it's tied directly to the games you're trying to watch. Add the broadcast TV fee most providers now bill separately, and the real monthly cost ends up higher than the number advertised when you signed up.

Then factor in equipment. Cable and satellite boxes are rented rather than owned, so a second TV in the house means a second monthly charge for as long as you keep the service, hockey season or not.

What IPTV costs instead

An IPTV subscription treats hockey the same as any other channel category, it's part of the lineup you're already paying for, with no separate sports tier and no regional network surcharge. You pay one flat rate and watch on a device you already own, a Firestick, an Android TV box, a Smart TV, or a laptop. Check current plan pricing for exact numbers, since those shift over time, but the billing stays simple in a way cable billing generally doesn't.

Where the cost gap actually comes from

It's rarely the base package price that makes the biggest difference, it's what gets added underneath it. Box rental per room, a regional sports fee, a broadcast fee, and a price increase once an introductory rate expires can combine into a bill well above what you originally agreed to pay. IPTV sidesteps most of this by design, since there's no equipment to rent and no separate sports add-on tier to sell you into.

Contract length matters too, especially for a sport with a long season followed by a quieter offseason. Cable plans often come with a term commitment and an early termination fee. Most IPTV subscriptions run month to month, so you're free to pause after the playoffs end and pick it back up again once the new season starts without eating a penalty.

A fair look at the tradeoffs

IPTV isn't without its downsides, and it's worth saying so directly. Your stream depends on your home internet connection rather than a dedicated cable line, so a weaker connection shows up faster as buffering, particularly during high-traffic playoff games when a lot of households are streaming at once. You're also using an app's on-screen guide instead of a cable remote, a change some people adjust to right away and others take a little longer with. For a solid internet connection, the savings tend to hold up consistently across a full season, playoffs included.

Why a long season changes the math

Hockey runs an 82-game regular season before the playoffs even begin, which means a lot of months where you're paying for TV access either way. That works against cable's fee structure, since box rental and regional sports surcharges are billed every single month regardless of how many games are actually on. A flat IPTV rate doesn't care whether it's a quiet Tuesday with one game or a packed Saturday with a full slate, so the longer the season runs, the more that flat structure tends to pay off compared to a bill full of stacked monthly fees.

Most regular-season hockey also airs on regional sports networks rather than big national broadcasts, which is exactly the coverage cable tends to charge a separate fee for. An IPTV lineup folds that regional coverage into the base package instead of treating it as a premium add-on.

Running the numbers across a full season

Stretch any of these recurring fees across seven or eight months of hockey and small monthly amounts turn into a real total by the time the playoffs wrap up. A box rental charge that seems minor on one statement adds up over a long season, and a regional sports fee does the same. You don't need to track this down to the dollar to see the pattern: a season this long rewards one flat monthly rate far more than it rewards a bill with several smaller charges stacked underneath the headline price.

Factoring in the playoffs

Once the postseason starts, a lot of hockey viewing shifts toward games that already air on channels you had access to during the regular season, so the coverage itself doesn't typically require anything new on your end. The cost side stays consistent too. Cable keeps billing the same package price plus box fees through the playoffs, and an IPTV subscription keeps charging the same flat monthly rate whether it's a quiet night in November or a tense elimination game in June. That predictability is one of the more underrated parts of the comparison, since it means your budget doesn't need to account for a cost spike just because the games got more important.

Getting set up

If you've already got a Firestick, an Android TV box, or a Smart TV with apps, the subscription is the whole cost, nothing else to buy. Coming from an older TV with no smart features just means a basic streaming stick, a small one-time purchase that's still far cheaper than a year of cable equipment fees.

Frequently asked questions

Is an IPTV subscription cheaper overall than a cable sports package for hockey?

In most cases yes, mainly because it avoids box rental fees and regional sports network surcharges that cable bills tend to add on separately.

Do I need to pay extra for regional sports networks with IPTV?

No, regional sports coverage is part of the standard channel lineup rather than a separate paid surcharge the way it often is on cable.

What equipment do I need to buy to watch NHL games through IPTV?

If you already own a Firestick, Android TV box, or Smart TV with apps, nothing extra. If not, a basic streaming stick is a small one-time purchase.

Does the cost change during the playoffs?

No, your IPTV subscription rate stays flat year round, unlike some cable sports add-ons that charge extra during the postseason.

Is there a contract with IPTV the way there is with cable or satellite?

No, most IPTV subscriptions are month to month with no term agreement, so there's no early termination fee if you decide to cancel.

Will my bill increase after the first few months like it does with cable?

No, the rate you sign up for is the rate you keep paying. Cable introductory pricing typically expires and resets to a higher standard rate.
